# Technical synthesis

A robot motion program can be technically correct but still fail in a sociotechnical system if people cannot easily understand or predict what the robot will do. For example, a hallway robot might follow the correct path, stay within its speed limits, and stop safely, but a person nearby could still feel uncomfortable if the robot begins turning without clearly signaling its intention. This could be especially important in crowded or shared spaces where pedestrians need enough time and space to react. Additional evidence would be needed from real users, such as observing how people respond to the robot, measuring passing distance and reaction time, and asking whether visual or audio turn signals make its motion easier to understand. This would help evaluate whether the robot is not only technically correct, but also safe and understandable around people.
